On every call.

The same nine things happen whether it is the first call of the morning or the one at 3 a.m. Nothing is ported and nothing is replaced. The system sits under the number you already print on the van.

  1. Answered on the line you already publish.The number on your trucks, your signage and your listing keeps ringing there.
  2. The caller hears that it is automated, in the first sentence.
  3. Name, callback number and address taken.
  4. The problem written down in the caller’s own words.Not summarised into a category.
  5. Urgency judged by the rules you set.You decide what counts as an emergency for your business and what waits until morning.
  6. Urgent calls routed to the on-call phone.By text, with the address and the access notes.
  7. Booked into your calendar.Only into windows you have opened.
  8. Confirmation texted to the caller.
  9. The record written to the systems you already run.Your CRM, your dispatch board, your calendar. Not a spreadsheet on the side.

Open 50 hours a week. Reachable 168.

A business that answers its phone 8 to 6, Monday to Friday, is reachable for 50 of the week’s 168 hours. For the other 118, the call goes to voicemail, and voicemail qualifies nobody.

A person reads the first calls.

Before the system runs on its own, a person at Nexus AI Horizon reviews the early calls: what was asked, what was booked, what should have been routed and was not. The rules get corrected on the transcript, not on a hunch. You can read every transcript and every rule yourself, and if you want it off, it goes off.
