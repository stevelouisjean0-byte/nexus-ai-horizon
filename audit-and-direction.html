00 · Summary

Three things outrank the redesign

The cinematic rebuild is worth doing, and the creative direction is below. But the audit surfaced problems that cost money today, before any pixel changes.

1. Your primary call-to-action goes to a parked domain

Eleven links on the homepage — including every “Book a strategy call” button — point at nexusaihorizon.com, not nxaihorizon.com. That domain is not your site. It serves a 114-byte page that redirects visitors to an advertising lander:

$ curl -sSL https://nexusaihorizon.com/contact/ <!DOCTYPE html><html><head><script> window.location.href="/lander" </script></head></html> HTTP 200 · Content-Length 114 · not your website

Both published email addresses — hello@nexusaihorizon.com and press@nexusaihorizon.com — sit on that same parked domain. Assume inbound email is not being delivered. Please check whether anything has ever arrived at those addresses; that answer sets the urgency on everything else.

This is a same-day fix and does not need to wait for the redesign.

2. Roughly twenty published numbers cannot be verified from outside

The site states 31 active deployments, a 1.8-second average answer time, a 62% first-call booking rate, 97% of calls answered under three seconds, 4.2× more booked jobs, $312K average recovered revenue, $4.6M new AUM for a named-by-industry RIA client, and “SOC 2-ready · CCO-approved · TCPA compliant.” None of these carry a source, a method, or a date on the page.

I have not removed or altered any of them. Section 13 lists every claim I found so you can mark each one verified, reworded, or removed. Anything unverified at build time gets replaced with a demonstration rather than a number — the redesign is stronger for it, because a working demo outperforms a statistic a visitor has no reason to trust.

3. The site is currently selling two different companies

The homepage sells AI voice and SMS for four service industries. The 85 city pages sell enterprise AI consulting — “air-gapped intelligence libraries,” “single-tenant neural networks,” “supply chain and fleet logistics.” They share a domain, a nav, and nothing else. A visitor who lands on /miami-fl/ from search and then clicks Home meets an unrelated business.

The redesign resolves this by making the homepage positioning the only positioning, and rebuilding the location layer as a genuine service-area directory.

What I did and did not do

This audit was read-only: I fetched the live HTML, both sitemaps, robots.txt, and the image and CSS assets. Nothing on the site was changed, deleted, or published. Every finding below cites what I actually retrieved. Where I could not measure something from outside — Lighthouse scores, analytics, backlinks, CRM wiring — I say so rather than estimating.

02 · Errors and risks

Findings register

Every item was observed in the live source on 3 September 2026. Severity reflects business impact, not effort. P1 items should be fixed now, independently of the redesign.

11 links target nexusaihorizon.com, which returns a 114-byte JavaScript redirect to /lander. Visitors clicking “Book a strategy call” leave your site and land on parking ads.

P1F-03
No privacy policy exists anywhere on the site

/privacy-policy/, /privacy/ and /terms/ all return 404. The contact form collects name, email, mobile and free text, and the site advertises SMS automation and states “TCPA compliant.” The form's own line “We never share your information” has no policy behind it.

FixRender the final value in the HTML and animate from it, so the correct number is the fallback rather than zero.
P2F-10
Albany page contains Syracuse copy

The Albany page carries the paragraph “At Nexus AI, we partner with Syracuse organizations… Downtown Syracuse… University Hill hospitals… Clay and Liverpool” directly beneath its Albany paragraph. The same page also breaks the word “New York” across a line as “New Yor / k's Capital District.”

FixSymptomatic of templated generation. Every location page needs a proofread before it survives the migration.
P2F-11
Exact-match keyword phrases stuffed into body copy

“AI Automation Services Miami FL” appears appended to six consecutive sentences on the Miami page. “AI Consulting Albany NY” four times on Albany. “AI Voice Agent Services Yonkers NY” five times on Yonkers. The homepage hero ends “No new hires AI Consulting Services USA .” — the phrase jammed mid-sentence with a stranded period.

FixRemove all of it. This is the pattern Google's spam policies name directly, and it is the reason the hero currently fails the five-second comprehension test.
P2F-12
All JavaScript is deferred until user interaction

Airlift moves every script into a delayed queue fired by mousemove, click, keydown, wheel or touch. It flatters synthetic scores but means counters, menus and any interactive module are inert until the visitor touches the page — and stay inert for a crawler.

FixKeep delay for genuinely non-critical scripts; exclude anything the first screen depends on. The cinematic build makes this decision load-bearing.

/homepage/ (“Homepage - Nexus Ai Horizon”) and /home-old1/ (“Main Home (old)”) both return 200, both carry index, follow, and both self-canonicalise. /home-old1/ is a near-copy of the live homepage, so it competes with your money page.

The homepage, About and footer all say Lower Manhattan, “Operating · Nationwide.” The city pages imply local presence in Birmingham, Charleston, Savannah, Tallahassee and 80 others. Which markets do you actually serve, and how do you want that described? The directory structure follows from that answer.

06 · Creative direction

The operations room at 2am

Not a chatbot startup, not a template. The reference point is the room where the calls are handled — dark, quiet, precise, with light used only where something is actually happening.

The idea

The site is built as a single continuous night shift. The visitor arrives at a dark operations surface, a call comes in, and they follow that one call all the way to a booked job and a confirmed customer. Everything else on the page is instrumentation around that call.

The discipline that keeps this from looking like every other dark AI site is restraint with light. The ground is genuinely dark and genuinely quiet. Accent colour is not decoration — it appears only where something is live: an active waveform, a signal in transit, a state that just changed, a call to action. If a colour is glowing, it means something. That rule alone will separate this from the category.

What we are not doing

  • No purple-to-blue gradient hero. No neon grid floor. No glassmorphic cards stacked on a blurred blob.
  • No stock imagery of people wearing headsets, and no humanoid-robot illustration.
  • No animation that exists because it is possible. Every motion cue in Section 9 maps to a step in the call.
  • No number on screen without a source beneath it.

Texture and depth

Surfaces are matte graphite, separated by hairline rules and very slight elevation rather than heavy shadow — closer to hardware than to software. Depth comes from layering: a slow parallax field of routed call paths well behind the content, a mid layer of interface panels, and type sitting crisply on top. Cursor movement shifts the deepest layer only, by a few pixels. On mobile that layer is a static image.

The demonstration is the hero of the page

Most sites in this category assert. This one plays a call and shows the work. Placing the sample call immediately after the hero is the single highest-conviction structural decision in this proposal: it is the fastest possible route from “what does this company do” to belief, and it does not depend on a statistic you have to defend.

Component standards

  • Buttons — primary is solid electric blue with dark label; secondary is hairline-outlined with warm white; both 48px minimum, 2px focus ring offset by 2px, and a label that names the outcome.
  • Cards — graphite surface, hairline border, 12px radius, 24–32px padding, 2px lift and a brighter border on hover. Never nested more than one deep.
  • Forms — visible label above every field, never placeholder-as-label. Error text below the field naming the problem and the fix. Success state replaces the form with the four-step “what happens next.”
  • Data displays — every figure carries a label, a unit, and a source line. A statistic without a source line is not a valid instance of the component.
  • Live indicators — 6px dot, cyan, slow pulse; paused entirely under reduced-motion, where it becomes a static dot with a text label.
  • Media — every image and video has explicit width and height to hold layout; video carries a poster; audio carries a transcript.

The Cost of Silence

The Cost of Silence
A missed call is not always a lost customer. But some of them are, and most businesses have never counted. Enter your numbers and see the range.

Formula shown on the page, in full: missed calls = monthly inbound × missed-call rate lost jobs = missed calls × conversion rate low estimate = lost jobs × average job value × 0.35 high estimate = lost jobs × average job value × 0.70 The 0.35–0.70 band assumes a share of missed callers call back or reach you another way. It is an assumption, and it is stated as one on the page.

Displayed beneath the result: “This is an estimate based on the figures you entered and the stated assumptions. It is not a prediction of revenue. Not every missed call would have become a paying customer.” This replaces the current fabricated ledger of 18 invented lost calls entirely.

09 · Motion storyboard

From Ring to Revenue

One pinned sequence, eight stages, one signal travelling through all of them. Total scroll distance roughly three viewport heights, scrubbed — the visitor controls the pace and can stop anywhere.

Stage 01 · 0–12%
Ring

An inbound call enters the system.

A single point of light arrives at the left edge and pulses. Concentric rings expand once and fade. The waveform sits flat and silent. Everything downstream is dimmed to 20% opacity.
Stage 02 · 12–25%
Answer

The AI voice agent answers within the approved response standard.

The flat line breaks into an active waveform. The status chip flips from Incoming to Connected in cyan. The dimmed pipeline lifts to 40%.
Stage 03 · 25–38%
Understand

The agent identifies the caller's need, location, urgency, and intent.

Transcript lines type in beneath the waveform. Four field chips — need, location, urgency, intent — fill one at a time as the words that triggered them are briefly underlined.
Stage 04 · 38–50%
Qualify

The system confirms the caller meets the business's service requirements.

The chips consolidate into a single qualification card. A checkmark draws in green. The signal, which has been holding at the node, brightens and begins to move.
Stage 05 · 50–63%
Route or Book

Urgent requests are routed. Standard appointments are scheduled.

The path forks. One branch pulses amber toward Dispatch; the other runs blue into a calendar that opens and takes a slot. Only the branch that matches the demo call illuminates — the other stays hairline.
Stage 06 · 63–75%
Update

The lead, transcript, and appointment enter the company's approved CRM.

The signal enters a panel labelled with the CRM logo. A record assembles line by line: contact, job type, urgency, transcript attached. Held one beat longer than the other stages — this is the one that closes the loop for an operator.
Stage 07 · 75–88%
Confirm

The customer receives an SMS confirmation.

A message bubble rises into a phone frame at the right edge, with a delivery tick. The waveform settles back to flat — the call has ended.
Stage 08 · 88–100%
Follow Up

An approved follow-up sequence continues until the customer responds, books, or opts out.

Three scheduled touches appear on a small timeline, then two dim as an inbound reply arrives and the sequence stops. The final frame shows the completed path, lit end to end, with the CTA resolving beneath it.

Motion rules

  • One library. GSAP with ScrollTrigger, plus Lenis for smoothing. Nothing else. No Framer Motion alongside it, no second scroll library.
  • Scrubbed, not triggered. The sequence is tied to scroll position, so scrolling back reverses it exactly. No autoplay, no scroll-jacking, no forced duration.
  • Transform and opacity only. Nothing animates layout. Anything moving continuously gets will-change and gives it back when idle.
  • Off-screen is paused. Canvas loops stop when their section leaves the viewport.
  • Reduced motion is a real path, not a switch. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the pin is released and all eight stages render as a static, fully readable list with the same words. Nothing is lost — the content was never carried by the animation alone.
  • Mobile simplifies rather than shrinks. No pinning below 900px; stages become sequential cards revealed on entry.

Other approved motion elsewhere: hero headline mask-reveal on load, section transitions at 420ms, card lift on hover, number roll-ups that start from the final value in the HTML (F-09), and a background parallax layer that responds to cursor position by no more than 8px.

10 · Technical approach

Rebuild on WordPress, not away from it

The recommendation is to keep WordPress and rebuild the front end as a proper theme, rather than moving platforms or continuing in Elementor.

Why this route

Elementor is the source of most of what is wrong here: the 39 stylesheets, the 372 KB of markup, the duplicated navigation, the “Cool Number” placeholder, the broken heading levels. A cinematic scroll sequence built inside it would fight the builder the whole way and stay slow. Moving to a headless or Next.js stack, on the other hand, means rebuilding your editing workflow, your hosting, and your SEO surface all at once — a much larger project than this brief needs, and it would put the migration at risk.

The middle path: a custom block theme on the existing WordPress install. You keep the CMS, the URLs, Rank Math, GA4, and your ability to edit. You lose the builder bloat.
